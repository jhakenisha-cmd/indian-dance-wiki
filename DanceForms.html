The Natya Shastra is the foundational encyclopedic treatise on the performing arts of ancient India.
Attributed to the sage Bharata Muni, it is often reverently referred to as the "Fifth Veda" because it combined
elements from the existing four Vedas to create a medium that was accessible and educational for all.

Key Aspects:

  • Authorship & Origin: Attributed to Bharata Muni, dated between 200 BCE and 200 CE, compiled from earlier traditions.
  • Content: Covers drama (Natya), music (Sangita), dance (Nritya/Nritta), poetics, and aesthetics, integrating elements from all four Vedas.
  • Rasa Theory: It's most profound contribution, explaining the emotional essence (Rasa) for the audience, identifying eight primary Rasas (love, humor, compassion, etc).
  • Abhinaya: Which is acting,it details physical expressions, gestures, speech, costumes, and stagecraft.
  • Music: Discusses vocal music, instruments (four types), swaras (notes), Jatis, and Murchanas, precursors to modern ragas.
  • Significance: A foundational text for Indian theatre, influencing dance and music across Asia, and recognized by UNESCO.
